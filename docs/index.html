Which X-ray
should come first?

AI can help prioritize suspected pneumonia. But what happens to the scan it gets wrong? We investigate the effect of model mistakes on a doctor's waiting line.

Our approach

From a prediction to a reading order.

We combine an image classifier with a simulation of one doctor reading chest X-rays. Our question is not just how accurate the model is, but who waits longer when its predictions are wrong.

01 · Score

Identify suspected pneumonia

Danyal fine-tuned ResNet-18 to produce a pneumonia score between 0 and 1 for each test X-ray.

02 · Simulate

Compare the waiting lines

Vova's simulation compares four reading-order rules, using the same scans and reading times within each run.

03 · Investigate

Look closely at mistakes

We measure delays for low-scoring pneumonia scans and investigate whether prioritizing overdue scans helps.

The workflow experiment

Same scans. Four different rules.

Rule Which scan goes next?
FIFO The oldest waiting scan. Our baseline.
SCORE The waiting scan with the highest pneumonia score.
SCORE + CAP The oldest overdue scan; otherwise the highest score.
PERFECT True pneumonia first. A reference using known labels.

Our design uses one doctor, 30 initial scans, an average arrival gap of 5 minutes and an average reading time of 4.5 minutes. Arrivals stop after eight hours; reading continues until the queue is empty.

We plan to summarize 500 runs and compare pneumonia shares of 10%, 25% and 60%, plus waiting thresholds of 30, 60 and 120 minutes. These settings are assumptions, not hospital measurements.

Queue results pending verification

Evidence before conclusions.

Verified queue charts will appear here after checking that the simulation uses the CSV from the reported model run. We do not yet claim that score ordering or the cap improves waits.

A cap raises priority; it does not guarantee a maximum wait. PERFECT is an idealized reference, not an optimum for every metric.

What we still need to learn

Limitations matter.

Who does the dataset represent?

Children aged 1–5 from one hospital in Guangzhou. Performance on adults or images from other hospitals remains unknown. We used 4,434 training, 782 validation and 624 test images.

How reliable are the missed-case experiments?

This model run missed one original pneumonia image. Repeatedly sampling its score cannot represent a wide range of real model failures.

What needs improving in validation?

Patient separation has not been checked. The uploaded notebook also applies random flips during validation; deterministic preprocessing should be used instead.

Does prioritization make the doctor faster?

No. It changes the reading order. Some scans may wait less while others wait longer. Simulation alone does not demonstrate better clinical outcomes.
